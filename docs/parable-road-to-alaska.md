# Parable: The Road to Alaska

## The Parable

I live in Delaware. Geographically, I am very far from Alaska.

I say I am getting to Alaska. Even if it is a stupid single-track unimproved trail, even if I am cutting bushes down to get through, I get to Alaska. I leave breadcrumbs so people can follow.

They trample the path. They improve the path. More people want to go to Alaska because I made it there and told them it is a good place to be. The vehicles get bigger. The roads get improved. Eventually, Alaska is no different than going to any other place.

## What It Means

This is not innovation. It is just **traversing**.

Going to Alaska is hard, but it is not interesting. The work is the going, not the cleverness. I do not go to Alaska to invent. I go because it has to be reached. The improvement of the path is a side effect of traversing it, not a goal.

## In Practice

- **End-to-end before paving.** Connect the endpoints with whatever works - rough, ugly, unimproved. Cargo can come later. The first traversal proves the route exists.
- **Ugly arrival beats elegant non-arrival.** A bushwhacked trail to Alaska is worth more than a beautifully designed highway that ends in Ohio.
- **Leave breadcrumbs.** The path I cut is for the people who follow. Document the route, even when the route is embarrassing.
- **The path improves itself.** Once the first traversal exists, others widen it, harden it, repave it. I do not have to build the highway. I have to make the highway *possible*.
- **The value of the first trip is the path, not the cargo.** I am not going to Alaska to deliver something. I am going to make Alaska reachable.

## How To Recognize This Move In My Work

When I push for an end-to-end skeleton over a deep vertical slice. When I accept a hardcoded value, a stub, or a manual step *just to close the loop*. When I refuse to optimize a leg of the journey before the whole journey works end-to-end once. When I document a working-but-ugly path instead of waiting until it is pretty.

That is the road to Alaska. Trampling improves it. Iteration improves it. The first traversal does not have to.

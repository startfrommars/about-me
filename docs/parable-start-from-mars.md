# Parable: Start From Mars

## The Parable

I advocate for AI at work, in a world where we are coding and creating. People look at me weird because of the way I talk to the LLM.

I tell it I want to go to Mars. I let it tell me no.

And when it tells me no, I do not start from Earth and work toward Mars. I start from Mars and work back.

I shoot for the impossible outcome early, because iteration and incrementalism are a workaround for a human deficit - the inability to articulate the shape. Maybe other people do not see that. But I do.

I play music. I make my own music. When I hear a song, I do not just hear the melody. I hear: there is a drop, then we are going to hit the bell on the ride cymbal on this note, here, and the swells, and the dynamism. I hear the mix.

That is a weird thing to say. But systems work the same way. I hear the mix. I see the shape.

So I know what Mars looks like. I shoot for Mars because the iterative, incremental human says: how do I get to Mars? I am going to buy some land, and I am going to make it flat, and then I am going to build this thing called a launchpad. But I need concrete. And then on that concrete, I need a structure. And that structure is going to hold a rocket. But I do not have a rocket yet, so I have to build the rocket. And then I need rocket fuel. And then this thing has to launch and actually get to Mars.

When I asked the AI, I did not say how to get to Mars. How do I know the rocket is even the right shape?

I just want to get to Mars. I have constraints. I want to get to Mars alive.

## What It Means

**State the destination as if it is already real. Let the model say no.**

The "no" is cheaper than my own incrementalism, because the model can see the whole sequence at once while I would be stuck reasoning launchpad-up.

Iteration and incrementalism are workarounds for the human inability to articulate shape. If I can articulate the shape, I skip the workaround.

**Constraints, not steps, are the spec.** "I want to get to Mars alive" is what makes the answer different. The constraints select among Mars-routes. The steps are downstream of that.

The hearing-the-mix part is the underlying capability. I perceive systems as a whole arrangement, not as a melody line. That is why I can state Mars first. Most people cannot, because they do not hear the mix.

## In Practice

- **Open with the destination, in present tense, fully formed.** "The system exposes X to consumer Y." Not "we will build X." The model gets to react to the shape, not to a sequence.
- **Let the model push back.** Disagreement is data. A "no, because..." is faster than ten turns of clarification.
- **Name constraints early.** Alive. Cheap. By Friday. Inside this account. With these credentials. The constraints carve the answer.
- **Resist the launchpad detour.** When I notice myself - or someone else - rationalizing concrete pours before the rocket has a shape, I name it. We are not on Mars yet. Mars first.
- **Trust the shape I hear.** If I hear the mix, I should say it, even if I cannot fully defend each instrument yet. The articulation is more useful than the certainty.

## How To Recognize This Move In My Work

When my first prompt is the end-state spec, not the next step. When I push back on "let's start by exploring options." When I refuse to design the launchpad until the rocket is defined. When I say "I just want to get to Mars" and treat that as a complete instruction.

That is starting from Mars. The route appears when the destination is fixed. The destination does not appear when the route is debated.
